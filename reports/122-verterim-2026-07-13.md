## C) Level and Strategy

### 1. Level Detected vs. Candidate’s Natural Level

The JD clearly targets a **mid-senior** engineer (4+ years required, reports to CTO, sets training architecture). The candidate’s natural level based on years is **junior-mid** (~2 years). However, the **depth** of work (production fine-tuning, distributed inference, multi-agent systems) is at an intermediate-senior level.

### 2. "Sell Senior Without Lying" Plan

- **Keywords to use in cover letter/resume:** "production-ready", "scaled deployment", "end-to-end", "multi-node GPU infrastructure", "inference optimization", "evaluation and monitoring".
- **Highlight metrics:** 40% throughput improvement via TensorRT-LLM, 35% latency reduction via Redis caching, 1,000+ requests/hour throughput.
- **Position founder/ownership:** Even as an employee, candidate owned RAG pipeline from prototype to scaled production in a regulated domain — that signals senior ownership.
- **Avoid** claiming years of experience; let the impact speak. Phrase as: "I bring 2 years of intensive hands-on work with production LLM systems that typically spans multiple roles."
- **Add a certificate or side project** that covers RLHF/DPO and evaluation suites to close the gap in the JD's preferred list.

### 3. "If They Downlevel Me" Plan

- **Accept** a title like "ML Engineer II" or "Associate Fine-Tuning Engineer" if the compensation is fair and the role offers direct CTO mentorship.
- **Negotiate** a 6-month review: "I'd like a formal milestone at 6 months where we evaluate my contributions against the senior expectations, with a clear path to title/promotion if I exceed."
- **Clarify promotion criteria** in the offer stage — ask what leveling looks like after 12 months and what concrete outcomes would warrant a step up.
- **Key driver:** the CTO reporting line is a massive career accelerant; downlevel might be worth it if the learning and visibility are extraordinary.
