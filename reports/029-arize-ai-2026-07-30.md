## C) Level and Strategy

**Level detected in JD:** Senior (4+ yr, "small senior team", "lead incident response", "mentor engineers")  
**Candidate's natural level:** Mid-level ML Engineer (2 yr exp)

**"Sell senior without lying" plan:**
- **Position as "AI Security Specialist"** — you have 2+ years of direct experience building production agentic systems, which gives you an insider's view of the attack surface. Few DevSecOps engineers understand the internals of LLM tool-use, prompt injection, and MCP agents.
- **Lead with the unique angle:** "I've deployed multi-agent systems that handle real user documents and financial queries. I know where the seams are — where an agent can leak data, where a tool call can be hijacked. I want to formalize that knowledge into security guardrails."
- **Founder/startup experience:** You built from prototype to production in a small team — that requires wearing multiple hats, including security thinking (even if you didn't call it that).

**"If they downlevel me" plan:**
- Accept if compensation is fair ($150k+ for mid-level would be a stretch — unlikely for this role).
- Negotiate 6-month review with clear promotion criteria (e.g., deliver X agentic security patterns, lead Y incident postmortems).
- Ask: "What would a mid-level engineer in this role own vs. a senior?"

**Reality check:** The experience gap and TypeScript absence are likely blockers. The company will have many candidates who check both boxes (DevSecOps + agentic AI interest). This is a high-risk, high-reward application.
